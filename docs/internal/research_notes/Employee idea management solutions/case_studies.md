# Case studies: how real organizations run employee suggestion / improvement-proposal programs

Scope note: research done 2026-11-11 with web search and fetch, in English, Russian and Japanese. Every figure below has the year it refers to. "Vendor claim" means the number comes from a software vendor or consultant that sells idea-management tools and was not independently checked. "Snippet" means the fact was seen only in a search-result excerpt and the page itself was not opened.

---

## 1. Classic models: Toyota, Japanese suggestion-system statistics, Dana, Milliken, Robinson & Schroeder

### Takeaway
The best-documented high-performing systems handle very large numbers of small ideas: Toyota about 50 per worker per year with about 98% implemented, Dana 24 per person per year at 80%, Boardroom 104 per year at over 90%, Brasilata about 150 per person per year at 90%. They share four traits: the immediate supervisor decides quickly, ideas are mostly ones the author can carry out within their own job, rewards are small or collective, and every idea gets an answer. Robinson & Schroeder's main empirical claim is that about 80% of an organization's improvement potential lies in front-line ideas. Their recurring finding is that the bottleneck is management's handling of ideas, not employees' creativity.

### Cited Findings

**Toyota: Creative Idea Suggestion System (創意くふう提案制度)**
- Origin. Eiji Toyoda saw Ford's reward-based suggestion system on a 1950 visit and Toyota adopted it in 1951. The motto "Good Thinking, Good Products" dates from 1953. — [Art of Lean TPS Encyclopedia](https://artoflean.com/reference/suggestion-system/); [Lean Enterprise Institute](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/recruiting-creative-ideas/). LEI adds that Toyoda later learned Ford had dropped its own system because it "didn't work".
- 1973 data point: about 43,000 employees submitted more than 250,000 suggestions in a year, and "well over half" were adopted. That is roughly 5.8 per employee (my arithmetic). — [Art of Lean](https://artoflean.com/reference/suggestion-system/)
- 1990 data point: Toyota's April 1992 booklet "The Toyota Production System" reportedly states that employees in Japan proposed nearly two million improvements in 1990 and that 97% were implemented. — [LeanBlog, 2026, quoting the 1992 booklet](https://www.leanblog.org/2026/06/kaizen-job-ownership-toyota-1992/) (snippet)
- Cumulative figure: an article titled "40 Years, 20 Million Ideas: The Toyota Suggestion System" puts the total at about 20 million ideas from 1951 to about 1991. — [Gale Academic OneFile record](https://go.gale.com/ps/i.do?id=GALE%7CA12124310&sid=googleScholar&v=2.1&it=r&linkaccess=abs&issn=00197939&p=AONE&sw=w) (title only)
- Practitioner account from about 2009 (NUMMI/Toyota experience):
  - A typical production worker submits about one suggestion a week, "50 or so" a year.
  - About 98% of suggestions are approved and about 98% implemented.
  - Office staff target: one implemented suggestion a month.
  - Process: the worker discusses the idea with the team leader (about 1 per 5–6 workers), gets agreement to try it, tries it, then writes it up and collects the reward. Feedback is "essentially immediate".
  - Suggestions are about the worker's own job, not general matters such as the cafeteria menu.
  - Rewards were mostly 500–2,000 yen (under US$10). 3,000 yen was "a major event".
  - Contrast: GM (about 1985) calculated a $20 processing cost per suggestion and required every suggestion to save at least $20.
  - Source: [LEI, "Recruiting Creative Ideas"](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/recruiting-creative-ideas/)
- Form and process:
  - A single A4 sheet with fields for the current situation, the condition before, the condition after, and the result. Handwriting is fine and the bar for an idea is deliberately low.
  - Employees get time during the workday to write proposals, and a supervisor reads and comments on every one.
  - Small improvements are approved at group-leader or supervisor level. Larger ones go up to a plant committee.
  - Rewards range from a few hundred yen to several hundred thousand yen for outstanding ideas.
  - Failure modes named: quotas or pressure, setting the bar too high, failing to respond to every proposal, and a generic idea box disconnected from standard work.
  - Source: [Art of Lean](https://artoflean.com/reference/suggestion-system/)
- Toyota tour guide claim, 2014 (unverified): about 430,000 kaizens implemented a year in Japan across about 68,000 employees, roughly 6.3 per employee. — [LeanBlog 2014](https://www.leanblog.org/2014/11/our-toyota-tour-guides-kaizen/)
- Weakly sourced recent claim: 810,000 ideas in 2023, 14.4 per person, about 70% implemented. This conflicts with the 95%+ figures above and could not be confirmed from a primary source. — [Ideawake vendor blog](https://ideawake.com/the-success-of-toyotas-employee-suggestion-program/) (snippet)

**Japanese national statistics (Japan Human Relations Association, JHRA / 日本HR協会)**
- JHRA and the Japan Management Association (日本経営協会) run the "改善・提案実績調査" (survey of improvement and suggestion results) every year. It has run since 1960 and is described as the only survey of its kind in Japan. — [hr-kaizen.com survey page](https://www.hr-kaizen.com/apps/note/report/)
- Sample size: the FY2024 tally covered 291 establishments (snippet). The FY2025 tally, collected in 2026, covered 277.
- Trend: the page says the number of improvement suggestions is declining while the economic effect per suggestion has risen sharply. It also says participation rose in the latest round.
- The per-person averages, adoption rates and the 20-year trend tables are in linked files I could not access. — [hr-kaizen.com](https://www.hr-kaizen.com/apps/note/report/)
- Historic contrast (1981 study mission, secondary source): Japanese companies were getting close to 50 written suggestions per employee per year, against one idea every seven years from the average American employee. One site (Fukushima) reported 570 per employee per year. — [MoldMaking Technology](https://www.moldmakingtechnology.com/articles/making-quick-and-easy-kaizen-work-for-your-shop)
- JHRA's framing, as reported by the same article:
  - The Japanese-style system stresses the morale benefit of participation. The American style stresses economic benefit and pays incentives.
  - Most suggestions with economic impact come from groups. Individual suggestions mainly serve morale and learning.
  - Source: [MoldMaking Technology](https://www.moldmakingtechnology.com/articles/making-quick-and-easy-kaizen-work-for-your-shop)

**Dana Corporation (as documented by Robinson & Schroeder)**
- In "Ideas Are Free" (2004), Dana had over 60,000 employees. Every employee was expected to submit two ideas a month, some facilities exceeded twice that, and the worldwide implementation rate was 80%. — [AbeBooks listing/excerpt](https://www.abebooks.com/9781576753743/Ideas-Free-Idea-Revolution-Liberating-1576753743/plp); [Everand excerpt](https://www.everand.com/book/677993595/Ideas-Are-Free-How-the-Idea-Revolution-is-Liberating-People-and-Transforming-Organizations)
- Dana's Cape Girardeau, Missouri plant had 300 people and implemented about 36 ideas per person per year. The plant manager said 80% of the operation's improvement came from front-line ideas, and this became the origin of the authors' "80/20 principle". — [The Idea-Driven Organization, sample chapter (2014)](https://ekkozvbwnx8.exactdn.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/IdeaDrivenOrganization_sampleChapter.pdf)
- Caveat: these Dana figures come from the authors' fieldwork (1990s–early 2000s), not from Dana's own audited data.

**Milliken & Company**
- I found no public primary source for Milliken's idea-system metrics, or for the widely repeated "acknowledge within 24 hours, act within 72 hours" rule. Searches turned up nothing to verify it, so treat it as folklore unless a book source is found (see Gaps).
- The only verifiable detail is from an older EHS Today article (about 2010s): "Opportunity for Improvement" (OFI) suggestions are submitted weekly by associates, reviewed at the Safety Steering Committee meeting and assigned an owner. — [EHS Today](https://www.ehstoday.com/safety/article/21915522/millikens-keys-to-employee-engagement-increased-workplace-safety-and-productivity)

**Robinson & Schroeder: "Ideas Are Free" (2004) and "The Idea-Driven Organization" (2014)**
- The best systems "routinely implement twenty, fifty, or even a hundred ideas per person per year". The authors argue that about 80% of an organization's improvement potential lies in front-line ideas and 20% in management-driven initiatives. — [IDO sample chapter](https://ekkozvbwnx8.exactdn.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/IdeaDrivenOrganization_sampleChapter.pdf)
- Brasilata (Brazilian steel-can maker): nearly 1,000 front-line employees, whose job title is "inventor", produce about 150,000 ideas a year, and 90% are implemented. The CEO took almost 20 years to build the system. — same source
- Clarion Hotel Stockholm:
  - More than 50 ideas per employee per year, about one a week.
  - Staff are trained to treat every guest complaint, question or confusion as an input.
  - Ideas go into a special computer application. If a staff member has no solution, they enter the raw problem.
  - Each department holds a weekly idea meeting to decide what to do with each idea and problem.
  - Source: same chapter
- Coca-Cola Stockholm bottling, 2007:
  - Two black-belt and five green-belt Six Sigma projects saved SEK 2.5M.
  - 1,720 front-line ideas saved about SEK 8M, 76% of the total. The share rose to 83% in 2008.
  - The unit was implementing 15 ideas per person per year.
  - It stopped tracking idea savings in 2010 because the benefit was clear.
  - Source: same chapter
- U.S. Navy technical support base:
  - Leadership budgeted $6.8M of Lean Six Sigma savings: 79.4% from management projects and 20.6% from front-line projects.
  - Actual savings were $1.2M from management projects (17.6%) and $5.6M from grassroots projects (82.4%).
  - Source: same chapter
- Boardroom Inc. (about 100 employees, U.S. publisher):
  - 104 ideas per employee per year, over 90% implemented.
  - Rule: an average of two ideas per week over each quarter. Missing it costs the quarterly bonus (averaging $3,000–4,000), and missing it twice means termination. No one had ever lost a bonus.
  - Ideas were built into every team's weekly meeting.
  - Source: [Dean Schroeder blog](https://dmschroeder.com/the-surprising-consequences-of-a-high-performance-idea-system/)
- Traits of a high-performing system (book summary):
  - Ideas are encouraged and easy to submit.
  - Evaluation is quick and effective, and feedback is timely, constructive and informative.
  - Implementation is rapid.
  - People are recognized.
  - The system itself is measured and improved.
  - Reward principles: base rewards on broad, aggregate measures of impact, share them equitably under public rules, and integrate them into how the company is run. Individual per-idea payments can undermine teamwork.
  - "Managers' willingness to listen is often the real bottleneck." Fix the handling of existing ideas before asking for more.
  - Source: [JFD Performance Solutions summary](https://www.jfdperfsolutions.com/book-summary-ideas-are-free-by-alan-robinson-dean-schroeder/)
- The authors' characterization of the traditional suggestion box: "where ideas go to die". The common pitfall they name is paying incentives for every idea submitted. — [search summary of Ideas Are Free reviews](https://www.scribd.com/document/318277825/Ideas-Are-Free-Alan-G-Robinson-Dean-M-Schroeder-Business-Summary) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The "classic" numbers come mostly from the 1970s to 2000s. The only current Japanese data (JHRA FY2024–25) says suggestion counts are falling while value per suggestion rises. This suggests mature Japanese systems are shifting from volume to quality.
- Across Toyota, Dana, Clarion and Boardroom, the high implementation rates are produced by design. Ideas are scoped to what the author or their team can do, and the direct supervisor approves them on the spot or at a weekly meeting. Implementation is high because ideas are small and decided locally, not because a central committee is generous.
- Toyota's yen-level rewards and Boardroom's collective bonus both show that large per-idea cash payments are not what drives volume. Visible response and recognition are.

### Gaps
- No primary JHRA per-person averages or adoption rates could be extracted. They sit in linked files on hr-kaizen.com.
- I found no verifiable source for Milliken's idea counts or its "24h/72h" response rule.
- No current (2020s) Toyota corporate figure from Toyota itself. The 2023 figures circulating online are vendor-sourced and conflict with older data.

## 2. Russian corporate programs: process design and numbers

### Takeaway
Large Russian industrial groups run formal "ППУ / Фабрика идей / Банк идей" systems that started as paper boxes and stands and moved to portals, mobile apps and chatbots (Severstal 2014, Gazprom Neft about 2018–19, EVRAZ 2021). Reported scale:
- Severstal: 414k submitted and 248k implemented over 7 years, more than 6.4bn RUB of effect (to 2019).
- EVRAZ: more than 100k in 2021, about 20bn RUB of effect.
- TVEL (Rosatom fuel company): more than 40k submitted and more than 35k implemented in 2025.

Typical process design:
- Review by local committees once or twice a month.
- Published local deadlines of 2–5 working days per step.
- Points-based or graded cash rewards of about 300–5,000 RUB, with large-effect ideas routed to a separate track.

Where submitted-versus-implemented figures were found, conversion ranged from about 6% (Gazprom Neft portal) to about 87% (TVEL).

### Cited Findings

**Severstal: «Фабрика идей» (interview 23.04.2019)**
- History: running since 2012, building on the company's earlier rationalization practice.
  - Before 2014: paper forms placed in boxes on shop-floor stands.
  - From 2014: a unified software platform «Фабрика идей» with a common idea database.
  - Source: [CFO Russia](https://www.cfo-russia.ru/stati/?article=50203)
- Review: each unit has a technical council (unit head plus key experts) that meets twice a month. It decides to implement, reject or send back for rework, and assigns a responsible expert to each idea. "Fast decision" and "feedback to the author" are named as core features, but no numeric deadline is stated. — same source
- Rewards:
  - Early: 500–2,000 RUB per idea, plus quarterly prizes (smartphones, tablets, tools).
  - From 2014: virtual points («фишки», 5–100 per accepted idea, scaled by economic effect), exchanged in a catalog of 35 items.
  - From 2018: quarterly certificates for the best ideas, worth 300 «фишек» (20,000 RUB).
  - Employees donated more than 1M RUB of points to charity.
  - Source: same source
- KPI lesson:
  - A KPI on the number of ideas with effect above 200,000 RUB, plus rewards for implementers, raised the program's effect about threefold.
  - When volume targets were cancelled in 2018, the number of submitted and implemented ideas fell, but economic effect doubled compared with 2017.
  - Source: same source
- Scale: more than 50,000 employees involved. The Russian Steel division receives more than 160 ideas a day and implements 95 a day. Over 7 years: 414,000 submitted, 248,000 implemented (about 60%), more than 6.4bn RUB effect. — same source

**EVRAZ: «Фабрика идей» / «Фабрика идей 2.0» (article 11.04.2022)**
- Volume: launched 2016 at about 1,000 proposals a year, tens of thousands in 2019, more than 100,000 in 2021. Since 2016: more than 270,000 submitted, about 140,000 implemented (about 52%). — [Neftegaz.RU](https://magazine.neftegaz.ru/articles/tsifrovizatsiya/733371-fabrika-idey-v-evraze-kak-predlozheniya-sotrudnikov-pomogayut-kompanii-zarabatyvat/)
- Effect: about 20bn RUB in 2021. — same source
- Digitization:
  - The digital version 2.0 went into industrial operation in spring 2021 after a 4-month pilot covering about 6,500 employees. By end-2021 it covered more than 71,000 employees.
  - It replaced Excel tables that "had become hard to manage at scale".
  - A corporate chatbot and mobile app let workers without a PC submit ideas.
  - Integration with BI and HR systems gives transparent reporting by division.
  - Source: same source
- Stages: draft, submission, expert review, voting, implementation decision, implementation control, recording of actual effect. — same source
- Problem board: a «Доска решения проблем» widget tracks deadlines, responsible owners and overdue problems. — same source
- Rewards: up to 10 average salaries depending on the effect, payments to the expert reviewers, contests with prizes, and an extra reward if the idea qualifies as a formal rationalization proposal. — same source

**Gazprom Neft: «Портал инициатив», Logistics, Refining & Sales block**
- Rollout: piloted at 2 plants in the first year and extended to all divisions in early 2019, after 14 months of development. — [IT HR Awards case page](https://ithrawards.ru/gazprom-neft-portal-iniciativ)
- Intake: PC or mobile, one single idea form, anonymous submission allowed. Accepted types:
  - an idea the author can implement alone
  - an idea that needs company resources
  - a problem statement inviting others to solve it
  - a request for help calculating the economic effect
  - Source: same source
- Review and statuses:
  - Experts score every idea on one point scale and must justify each verdict in writing.
  - Authors can appeal a rejection, and ideas can be escalated.
  - Authors track status at every stage in a personal account.
  - Source: same source
- Rewards: material rewards, training or recognition, and a lunch with the plant director. An idea-leader rating brings top authors to head office in St. Petersburg. — same source
- Numbers (date not stated, about 2019–2020):
  - 13,609 of about 15,000 employees registered (96%).
  - 4,781 ideas submitted, 2,561 recommended, 290 implemented (about 6% of submitted).
  - More than 2bn RUB profit attributed.
  - Source: same source

**SIBUR: «Улучшение малыми шагами»**
- SiburTyumenGaz implemented 853 employee ideas in 2026 year-to-date, with 983M RUB direct effect (article 22.10.2026). Ideas are proposed at the workplace, evaluated, and the best put into production. — [Energyland](https://energyland.info/news-show--neftegaz-288530)

**Sberbank: «Биржа идей» (2009–2012; today «СберИдея»)**
- Launch: piloted from November 2009 and launched permanently in October 2010. — [Kommersant, 21.10.2010](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/1525766)
- Participation (end of September 2010): about 40% of employees (89,500) registered and about 29,500 proposals submitted. — same source
- Savings: 650M RUB realized, about 1bn RUB expected by Q1 2011. — same source
- Cost and rewards: about 17M RUB to build and run. Rewards ranged from 30,000 to 350,000 RUB, scaled to the size of the innovation, with a 6M RUB reward budget. — same source
- Later figures, from search summaries of Kommersant/banki.ru coverage (snippet, 2011–2012):
  - Second year (October 2010 to October 2011): 7bn RUB savings and 8.1M RUB paid to authors.
  - Cumulative: 13bn RUB, more than 200,000 participants, more than 120,000 proposals.
  - Sources: [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/1788428); [banki.ru](https://www.banki.ru/news/daytheme/?id=2353255)
- No current SberIdea statistics were found.

**Rosatom: Rosatom Production System (ПСР), «ППУ» = предложения по улучшению**
- TVEL fuel company enterprises, 2025: more than 40,000 ППУ submitted, more than 39,000 accepted, more than 35,000 implemented (about 87%). Cumulatively since launch: about 803,000 ППУ and 5,100 ПСР projects. — [TVEL](https://www.tvel.ru/activity/rosatom-production-system/)
- TVEL earlier years (snippet): 2023, more than 36,000 submitted and more than 30,000 implemented. ППУ economic effect in 2022 was 390.3M RUB, against 9.4bn RUB from ПСР projects. — [TVEL ECP](https://ecp.tvel.ru/primary-activity/rosatom-production-system/)
- Electrochemical Plant (ЭХЗ): 64,881 ППУ with 382.8M RUB effect over the whole ПСР period, as of about 2018. — [up-pro.ru](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/systems/10yearpsr/)
- Mining and Chemical Combine (ГХК), step-by-step instruction for employees (27.10.2020). Source for all of the following: [up-pro.ru](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/systems/poshagovaya-instruktsiya-dlya-sotrudnikov/)
  - Intake: a paper form or Word template. Collection boxes or registration logs sit next to the ПСР information stand in each unit.
  - Review: the unit ПСР working group reviews within 5 days of submission, then the labor organization and pay department.
  - Rejection: the responsible group member informs the author and explains why.
  - Accepted ideas: the implementation order goes to the executing group within 3 working days.
  - Rewards by points: 3 pts = 1,000 RUB, 4 = 1,250, 5 = 1,500, 6 = 2,000, 7 = 2,500, 8 = 3,000, 9 = 5,000 (before regional coefficients). Criteria: conditional annual effect, ease of implementation, replicability.
- Other published regulations (snippet; not opened):
  - A manager who receives a proposal must pass it to the coordinator within 2 working days (described as a Rosatom regulation).
  - At Tochmash, results of review reach authors within 2 working days.
  - Source: [up-pro.ru library of regulations](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/organization_files/polozhenie-o-podache/)

**«Химремонт» / УК «ТАУ НефтеХим» (Tatarstan petrochemical maintenance): kaizen-proposal regulation with a full SLA chain**
- Source for all bullets: [up-pro.ru](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/organization_files/upravlenie-uluchsheniyami/)
- Intake: proposals go into a «Банк предложений» box or the corporate website. Anonymous proposals are not accepted.
- Steps and deadlines:
  - A coordinator collects proposals twice a month, registers them in a log, and sends the author an SMS.
  - A preliminary commission comments within 2 working days.
  - The main kaizen commission meets twice a month.
  - The decision protocol and an SMS to the author go out within 5 working days.
  - The executor gets the task within 2 working days.
  - Information requests must be answered within 7 working days.
  - Each approver of a feasibility study (ТЭО) has 3 working days.
  - Disputes are resolved by the commission chair within 14 calendar days. A curator hotline exists.
- Rewards:
  - 300–3,000 RUB per proposal, paid whether or not it is implemented.
  - The author's direct manager gets 20% of the quarterly author rewards.
  - The implementer gets 50% of the author's reward.
  - High-saving ideas are routed to formal rationalization rewards.
- No automatic escalation for missed deadlines is defined.

**PGK (First Freight Company, about 3,000 employees): «Банк идей» on a ticket system (Habr, 14.03.2023)**
- Source for all bullets: [Habr / FreightOne](https://habr.com/ru/post/722254)
- More than 6,000 initiatives submitted. Each initiative is a card with goal, problem and timeline.
- Approval: 5 stages, from profile departments through finance to the expert council. A configurable approver matrix routes cards and is maintained by non-IT staff.
- Deadlines:
  - Each initiative has an overall deadline plus a deadline per stage (for example "5 days").
  - If a stage overruns, the system shows which stage caused the delay.
  - Meeting deadlines is a KPI for the team.
- After implementation:
  - Authors enter the actual effect monthly, and the system sends automatic reminders when data is missing.
  - A built-in calculator with 30 approved methods replaced manual Excel calculations, which had frequent errors.

**Unnamed Russian industrial group (more than 40 enterprises), via its software developer (vendor claim)**
- Source for all bullets: [Sostav / Atvinta](https://www.sostav.ru/blogs/266473/57449)
- The ecosystem is split by idea size:
  - «Фабрика идей» for effects under 1M RUB
  - a "transformational proposals" service for effects above 1M RUB
  - a «Доска решения проблем» (problem board) with 20,000 registered problems
  - an expert community
- Totals and claim: 73,000 ideas and problems in total. The vendor claims "+20bn RUB to profit" from the large-idea track; no year is given.
- What digitization changed:
  - Ideas moved from paper and whiteboards to one shared database.
  - Responsible people are notified at once.
  - Real-time dashboards replaced hand-compiled reports.

**Other Russian data points**
- Transneft-Verkhnyaya Volga, 2024: more than 630 ideas, 160 implemented (about 25%). Authors received thank-you letters. — [Rambler/Finance](https://finance.rambler.ru/business/54026452-160-predlozheniy-po-optimizatsii-proizvodstva-bylo-vnedreno-ao-transneft-verhnyaya-volga-v-2024-godu/)
- Norilskgazprom (snippet): 60 initiatives in 2022 and 10 in 2023; 21 implemented in 2022. — [ngaz.ru](https://www.ngaz.ru/press-tsentr/news/proizvodstvennye-initsiativy/)
- National baseline (HSE ISSEK survey, 2023/24; snippet): only 11% of Russian employees had voiced any idea at work in the previous three years. — [HSE ISSEK](https://issek.hse.ru/mirror/pubs/share/1026160741.pdf)

### Inferences
- Russian programs that report high implementation rates (TVEL about 87%, Severstal about 60%) take mostly small, locally implementable ППУ. Gazprom Neft's portal, which invites resource-heavy ideas and problem statements, implemented about 6% of submissions. The type of idea accepted drives the implementation rate more than the software does.
- Severstal's 2018 experience is a warning against volume quotas. Removing them cut the count but doubled the value.
- Every mature program separates small ideas (decided locally, fast, small fixed reward) from big ones (business case, finance sign-off, effect-linked reward). EVRAZ, the unnamed holding and PGK all do this.
- The published SLAs are local and procedural: 2 days to forward, 5 days to decide or record, 2–3 days to hand over to an executor. Twice-monthly committees mean the effective wait to a decision is up to about 2–3 weeks.

### Gaps
- RZD (Банк идей / rationalization), KAMAZ, GAZ (Производственная система ГАЗ), Tatneft, Nornickel group-level, X5 and VTB: searches returned no usable program statistics or process descriptions. Their programs may exist but are not documented in accessible sources.
- No current SberIdea figures (post-2012).
- No Russian source states a corporate-wide numeric SLA for author feedback beyond the local regulations above.

## 3. Kazakhstan: which companies actually have documented programs

### Takeaway
ERG is the only Kazakhstan group found with a well-documented, high-volume program. Its «Фабрика идей» received 32,084 proposals in 2021 and paid 565M KZT for the best ideas, alongside the ERG Way lean system. KEGOC and KazTransOil, both state-related, document small rationalization and improvement-proposal activity. A KazTransOil example comes from the Pavlodar oil pipeline division. For Kaspi, Halyk, Kazakhtelecom, Air Astana, Kazatomprom and KazMunayGas, no employee-idea program with figures was found.

### Cited Findings
- **ERG (Eurasian Resources Group)**, from an article dated 03.02.2022. Source for all bullets: [Inbusiness.kz](https://inbusiness.kz/ru/news/svyshe-32-tysyach-proektov-po-uluchsheniyu-rabochih-processov-predlozhili-sotrudniki-eurasian-recourses-group-v-2021-godu)
  - «Фабрика идей» collected 32,084 proposals in 2021 and more than 62,000 over 4 years.
  - The article projects nearly 6bn KZT of total effect; this is a projection, not a result.
  - 565M KZT was paid to participants for the best ideas in 2021.
  - An "improvement fund" equal to 10% of the actual effect of large initiatives goes back to the originating enterprise and may be spent only on working conditions.
  - Under ERG Way, the lean production system started in 2019:
    - 2,500 employees sit on kaizen teams, which solved more than 2,000 production tasks in 2021.
    - Every section rated "good" in management walkthroughs gets a bank card with 300,000 KZT for working-condition improvements; 72M KZT was credited in 2021.
    - 2,000 employees were trained in lean in 2021, with 8,000 more planned.
- ERG's earlier figures (snippet, 2020 reporting): about 10,000 ideas a year, and about US$68M saved in 2019 from operational-efficiency measures proposed by employees. A separate external innovation portal (erg-innovation.kz) received about 100 proposals from outside innovators in 2019. — [Kapital.kz](https://kapital.kz/tehnology/91143/erg-otkryla-spetsial-nyy-sayt-dlya-innovatsionnykh-predlozheniy.html); [Tengrinews](https://tengrinews.kz/money/gruppa-kompaniy-erg-obyyavila-poisk-novyih-idey-i-tehnologiy-419773/)
- **KEGOC** (national grid operator, Samruk-Kazyna): reviews employee ideas every year and rewards the best materially. 254 rationalization proposals have been implemented since 2013, per a press release from about 3 years ago. — [KEGOC press release](https://www.kegoc.kz/ru/press-center/press-releases/162082/)
- **KazTransOil** (KMG group), from an article dated 17.07.2024. Source: [Bizmedia.kz](https://bizmedia.kz/2024-07-17-kaztransojl-sekonomili-okolo-40-mln-tenge-na-elektronagrevatelyah/)
  - Since 2023 the company has implemented 10 employee improvement proposals and 1 rationalization proposal.
  - One of them, new electric heaters at the Priirtyshsk facility, saves about 38.45M KZT a year. It was proposed by the chief power engineer of the Pavlodar oil pipeline division.
- **Samruk-Kazyna**:
  - Its Transformation Programme (from 2014) includes Lean and Six Sigma. — [Samruk-Kazyna Transformation Programme](https://sk.kz/upload/iblock/228/22883859332cf55e27b5fc895899cb0b.pdf)
  - Its corporate safety-management standard includes a tool for employees and contractors to report unsafe conditions and propose improvements to working conditions and equipment. — [SK safety standard](https://sk.kz/about-fund/safety/safety-management-corporate-standart-ru.pdf)
  - No fund-wide idea-program statistics were found.
- **Kaspi, Halyk, Kazakhtelecom, Air Astana:** no employee-suggestion program found. Halyk's documented platform, Skill Based, is for competency assessment, and Kazakhtelecom's CREDO pilot is about corporate values. — [Bes.media on Halyk](https://bes.media/news/ot-testirovaniya-do-nayma-halyk-bank-predstavil-sobstvennuyu-platformu-na-baze-ii/); [Forbes.kz on Kazakhtelecom](https://forbes.kz/articles/kazahtelekom_podvel_itogi_pilotnogo_proekta_po_transformatsii_korporativnyih_tsennostey_credo)

### Inferences
- In Kazakhstan, structured employee-idea programs exist mainly in heavy industry and quasi-state companies, and most come from a lean or production-system tradition. For a 15-year-old private distributor and integrator like Modus Trade, a lightweight system would be ahead of most local private-sector peers. The pitch can use local reference points: ERG, KEGOC, and KazTransOil in Pavlodar.
- ERG's "10% of effect back to the originating unit, spent on working conditions" is a collective-reward design that matches Robinson & Schroeder's advice on aggregate rewards.

### Gaps
- No program data for Kazatomprom, KazMunayGas at group level, Kaspi, Halyk, Kazakhtelecom or Air Astana. This is absence of evidence, not proof that none exist.
- I did not find the government «Бережливое производство» or national productivity initiatives in Kazakhstan with idea-system metrics (not searched in depth).
- No current (2023–2026) ERG Idea Factory statistics.

## 4. Mid-size and small company examples (closer to Modus Trade's scale)

### Takeaway
Documented small and mid-size cases are rare and come mostly from Robinson & Schroeder's fieldwork and vendor stories. The best evidence: Boardroom (about 100 people, 104 ideas per person per year), the Dana Cape Girardeau plant (300 people, 36 per person per year), the Clarion hotel (more than 50 per person per year), Coca-Cola Stockholm (15 per person per year), and PGK (about 3,000 people, more than 6,000 initiatives, digital stage deadlines). The recurring mechanism at small scale is a weekly team or department meeting where every idea gets a decision, not a central committee.

### Cited Findings
- **Boardroom Inc.** (about 100 people): 104 ideas per employee per year, more than 90% implemented. The quota is two ideas a week averaged over a quarter, tied to a quarterly bonus of $3,000–4,000. Ideas are integrated into weekly team meetings. — [Schroeder](https://dmschroeder.com/the-surprising-consequences-of-a-high-performance-idea-system/)
- **Dana, Cape Girardeau plant** (300 people): about 36 implemented ideas per person per year, and the plant manager attributed 80% of improvement to front-line ideas. — [IDO sample chapter](https://ekkozvbwnx8.exactdn.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/IdeaDrivenOrganization_sampleChapter.pdf)
- **Clarion Hotel Stockholm** (a single hotel): more than 50 ideas per employee per year, entered in an app. Raw problems are allowed without a solution, and each department holds a weekly idea meeting. — same source
- **Coca-Cola Stockholm** (one bottling unit): 15 ideas per person per year. In 2007, 1,720 ideas produced 76% of savings. — same source
- **PGK** (about 3,000 employees): more than 6,000 initiatives on a ticket-system «Банк идей» with per-stage deadlines and delay attribution (2023). — [Habr](https://habr.com/ru/post/722254)
- **Trinity Industries** (vendor claim): moving a shop-floor idea board to a digital platform took the team from 4 ideas to 653 ideas in six months. — [KaiNexus](https://blog.kainexus.com/customer/bringing-ideas-to-life-trinity-industries)
- **St. Clair Hospital** (vendor claim): 45% of submitted ideas resulted in change in the first year on a digital platform. — [KaiNexus](https://www.kainexus.com/when-post-its-didnt-cut-it-anymore-hospital-adopted-kainexus)
- **FastCap** (Paul Akers, "2 Second Lean", small U.S. maker and distributor of woodworking tools):
  - Every employee is expected to make a small improvement every day, and Akers asks operators and office staff alike to improve continuously. — [LeanBlog interview, 2012](https://www.leanblog.org/2012/01/how-paul-akers-built-culture-daily-improvement-2-second-lean/)
  - "Over 2,000 implemented improvements per year" appears only on a low-quality aggregator and is unverified.

### Inferences
- For organizations of fewer than 300 people, every documented success makes the regular team meeting the decision point: Boardroom weekly, Clarion weekly per department, Toyota at the team-leader conversation. None uses a separate review bureaucracy. That suits Modus Trade's department structure (sales, purchasing, design/engineering, IT, service, accounting).
- Clarion's "enter the raw problem if you have no solution" is directly relevant to the owner's pain. Issues and ideas go into one intake, and a problem without a proposed fix is still accepted.

### Gaps
- I found no documented case of a B2B distributor, security-systems integrator or IT-services company of 50–300 people with published idea-program metrics, in any country. Vendor case studies exist but rarely give size and before/after data together.

## 5. How organizations handle the "lost request" problem (deadlines, escalation, dashboards, "you said, we did")

### Takeaway
Programs that avoid silent loss combine five mechanisms:
1. Every submission is registered in a log or database with an owner.
2. Each step has a hard deadline of a few working days, and the author is notified at each step (SMS, email, personal account).
3. A recurring decision meeting clears the queue (weekly or twice a month).
4. Dashboards and boards show overdue items, and stage delays are attributed to a specific owner.
5. Every outcome, including rejection, comes back to the author with reasons.

The failure pattern is well documented. A large funnel of submitted ideas turns into a trickle of implemented ones, and employees then stop submitting (the "idea desert").

### Cited Findings
- **Silent-loss failure mode (quantified, vendor-reported):**
  - A hospital's ideas had about $20M of estimated value submitted, about $1M approved, and about $70K implemented.
  - The visible gap teaches staff that input doesn't lead to action, so submissions dry up ("idea desert").
  - Recommended metrics: the gap between submitted and implemented ideas and its trend, the share of approved ideas actually implemented, and whether implementation is concentrated in a few people (a fragility signal).
  - Source: [KaiNexus, Graban & Jacobson](https://www.kainexus.com/mark-graban-and-greg-jacobson-on-why-most-idea-programs-stall-and-how-to-close-the-gap-between-ideas-submitted-and-improvements-actually-implemented)
  - Vendor claim: only 2–3% of ideas in a typical suggestion box are implemented. — [KaiNexus](https://www.kainexus.com/suggestion-box)
- **Response deadlines in published Russian regulations:**
  - Химремонт/ТАУ НефтеХим:
    - Registration plus SMS on intake.
    - Preliminary comments within 2 working days.
    - Decision protocol plus SMS within 5 working days.
    - Executor tasked within 2 working days.
    - Disputes resolved within 14 calendar days, with a hotline.
    - Source: [up-pro.ru](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/organization_files/upravlenie-uluchsheniyami/)
  - ГХК/Rosatom: review within 5 days and hand-off to an executor within 3 working days. A rejection must be explained to the author. — [up-pro.ru](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/systems/poshagovaya-instruktsiya-dlya-sotrudnikov/)
  - Other regulations (snippet): forward to the coordinator within 2 working days; review results to the author within 2 working days (Tochmash). — [up-pro.ru](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/organization_files/polozhenie-o-podache/)
- **Stage deadlines with delay attribution:** PGK's system has an overall deadline plus per-stage deadlines (for example 5 days) and shows which department caused the slip. Deadline compliance is a team KPI, and monthly auto-reminders cover missing post-implementation data. — [Habr](https://habr.com/ru/post/722254)
- **Overdue tracking:** EVRAZ's «Доска решения проблем» tracks deadlines, responsible owners and overdue resolution, and BI dashboards report by division. — [Neftegaz.RU](https://magazine.neftegaz.ru/articles/tsifrovizatsiya/733371-fabrika-idey-v-evraze-kak-predlozheniya-sotrudnikov-pomogayut-kompanii-zarabatyvat/)
- **Status transparency, justified verdicts and appeal:** Gazprom Neft authors track status in a personal account, experts must justify every verdict, and authors can appeal rejections. Management gets data on how each division handles initiatives. — [IT HR Awards](https://ithrawards.ru/gazprom-neft-portal-iniciativ)
- **Fixed decision cadence:** Severstal's unit technical councils meet twice a month and assign a responsible expert per idea, with feedback to the author as a stated feature. — [CFO Russia](https://www.cfo-russia.ru/stati/?article=50203). Clarion's departments review all ideas and problems weekly. — [IDO sample chapter](https://ekkozvbwnx8.exactdn.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/IdeaDrivenOrganization_sampleChapter.pdf)
- **Every idea gets a response:** Toyota names "failing to respond to every proposal" as a failure mode, and a supervisor reads and comments on every one. — [Art of Lean](https://artoflean.com/reference/suggestion-system/). In LEI's account, feedback at Toyota is "essentially immediate" because the team leader agrees on the spot. — [LEI](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/recruiting-creative-ideas/)
- **Responding to an idea you can't adopt:** KaiNexus ranks the possible responses from worst to best:
  1. ignore it
  2. reject it outright
  3. explain why it can't be done as proposed
  4. find something that addresses the underlying problem, even partially
  - Source: [KaiNexus](https://www.kainexus.com/mark-graban-and-greg-jacobson-on-why-most-idea-programs-stall-and-how-to-close-the-gap-between-ideas-submitted-and-improvements-actually-implemented)
- **"You said, we did":**
  - Practitioner guidance: publish a tracker that pairs each theme raised with an owner, an action and a status. Be explicit about what is not being done and why, and close the loop on every item, including those not acted on.
  - This comes from HR/engagement consultancies and survey vendors, not from audited case studies.
  - Sources: [Flimp playbook](https://flimp.net/you-said-we-did-the-bottom-up-comms-playbook-that-builds-trust-fast/); [People Insight](https://peopleinsight.co.uk/you-said-we-did/); [SurveyMonkey](https://www.surveymonkey.com/learn/employee-feedback/closing-the-employee-feedback-loop/)
- **Unverified survey claim:** 93% of U.S. employees make suggestions regularly, but only 39% of companies have processes to address them. I could not trace this to the original SHRM survey. — [Suggestion Ox blog](https://suggestionox.com/set-up-suggestion-box) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The owner's specific fear, "employee thinks it's being handled but it's silently forgotten", is solved in practice by making status visible to the author and making overdue items visible to the boss. Every digitized program above (Gazprom Neft, EVRAZ, PGK, the Atvinta-built system) cites exactly these two features as what changed.
- Deadlines in the regulations are short (2–5 working days per step), but none of the published regulations define automatic escalation when a deadline is missed. PGK's delay attribution is the closest. Automatic escalation to the owner is a gap that a small-company tool could fill.

### Gaps
- No primary-source, audited before/after study showing that a specific SLA (for example "answer within 5 days") changed participation. The evidence is practitioner and vendor testimony.
- The Milliken "24h/72h" rule could not be sourced.

## 6. Lessons for Modus Trade: what transfers to a company of tens to a few hundred people, and what only works for giants

### Takeaway
What transfers:
- One intake for both problems and ideas.
- Registration with a named owner.
- Short, visible deadlines.
- A weekly or bi-weekly decision slot inside existing department meetings.
- Mandatory reasoned answers.
- An owner-level dashboard of open and overdue items.
- A public "you said, we did" feed.
- Modest, mostly non-cash recognition.

What does not transfer: multi-level expert councils, points stores, effect calculators with dozens of methods, BI/HR integrations, and per-idea cash scaled to large economic effects. These are built for 15,000–70,000-employee groups.

### Cited Findings (practices with evidence, mapped to scale)
- **Transfers well**
  - Single intake that accepts raw problems as well as ideas, with no solution required. — Clarion ([IDO](https://ekkozvbwnx8.exactdn.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/IdeaDrivenOrganization_sampleChapter.pdf)); Gazprom Neft accepts problem statements and "help me calculate" requests ([IT HR Awards](https://ithrawards.ru/gazprom-neft-portal-iniciativ))
  - Decisions taken in a regular team meeting. — Boardroom weekly, about 100 people ([Schroeder](https://dmschroeder.com/the-surprising-consequences-of-a-high-performance-idea-system/)); Clarion weekly per department ([IDO](https://ekkozvbwnx8.exactdn.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/IdeaDrivenOrganization_sampleChapter.pdf)); Severstal twice a month per unit ([CFO Russia](https://www.cfo-russia.ru/stati/?article=50203))
  - Short step deadlines (2–5 working days) with notification to the author at intake and at decision. — [Химремонт regulation](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/organization_files/upravlenie-uluchsheniyami/); [ГХК](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/systems/poshagovaya-instruktsiya-dlya-sotrudnikov/)
  - Every rejection explained, and authors can see status at any time. — [ГХК](https://up-pro.ru/library/production_management/systems/poshagovaya-instruktsiya-dlya-sotrudnikov/); [Gazprom Neft](https://ithrawards.ru/gazprom-neft-portal-iniciativ)
  - Per-stage deadlines that show who is holding an item. — [PGK](https://habr.com/ru/post/722254)
  - Small or symbolic rewards: Toyota's 500–2,000 yen, thank-you letters at Transneft, lunch with the plant director at Gazprom Neft. — [LEI](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/recruiting-creative-ideas/); [Rambler](https://finance.rambler.ru/business/54026452-160-predlozheniy-po-optimizatsii-proizvodstva-bylo-vnedreno-ao-transneft-verhnyaya-volga-v-2024-godu/); [IT HR Awards](https://ithrawards.ru/gazprom-neft-portal-iniciativ)
  - Collective reward tied to results, for example ERG's 10% of effect returned to the originating unit for working conditions. — [Inbusiness.kz](https://inbusiness.kz/ru/news/svyshe-32-tysyach-proektov-po-uluchsheniyu-rabochih-processov-predlozhili-sotrudniki-eurasian-recourses-group-v-2021-godu); in line with the [Robinson & Schroeder reward principles](https://www.jfdperfsolutions.com/book-summary-ideas-are-free-by-alan-robinson-dean-schroeder/)
  - Tracking submitted against implemented as the health metric. — [KaiNexus](https://www.kainexus.com/mark-graban-and-greg-jacobson-on-why-most-idea-programs-stall-and-how-to-close-the-gap-between-ideas-submitted-and-improvements-actually-implemented)
  - Mobile or chat intake for staff away from a desk; at Modus Trade that means service and installation engineers. — EVRAZ chatbot and app ([Neftegaz.RU](https://magazine.neftegaz.ru/articles/tsifrovizatsiya/733371-fabrika-idey-v-evraze-kak-predlozheniya-sotrudnikov-pomogayut-kompanii-zarabatyvat/))
- **Use with caution**
  - Volume quotas. Severstal's KPI first raised the effect, but dropping the targets later doubled value while volume fell ([CFO Russia](https://www.cfo-russia.ru/stati/?article=50203)). Toyota lists quotas as a failure mode ([Art of Lean](https://artoflean.com/reference/suggestion-system/)). Boardroom's hard quota worked, but in a 100-person firm with an owner personally driving it ([Schroeder](https://dmschroeder.com/the-surprising-consequences-of-a-high-performance-idea-system/)).
  - Per-idea cash scaled to economic effect, as at Sber in 2010 (30–350k RUB) and EVRAZ (up to 10 salaries). These need effect calculation and a finance sign-off, which is overhead a small firm can't carry. — [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/1525766); [Neftegaz.RU](https://magazine.neftegaz.ru/articles/tsifrovizatsiya/733371-fabrika-idey-v-evraze-kak-predlozheniya-sotrudnikov-pomogayut-kompanii-zarabatyvat/)
- **Giants only**
  - Five-stage approval routes through finance and an expert council, and effect calculators with 30 methodologies. — [PGK](https://habr.com/ru/post/722254)
  - Points stores with catalogs. — [Severstal, 35 items](https://www.cfo-russia.ru/stati/?article=50203)
  - BI and HR integration across 40+ enterprises. — [Atvinta/Sostav](https://www.sostav.ru/blogs/266473/57449)
  - Expert-reviewer payments. — [EVRAZ](https://magazine.neftegaz.ru/articles/tsifrovizatsiya/733371-fabrika-idey-v-evraze-kak-predlozheniya-sotrudnikov-pomogayut-kompanii-zarabatyvat/)

### Inferences
- **Realistic targets for an MVP pitch.** Mature small systems reach 15–100 implemented ideas per person per year. A starting company should not promise that. A believable first-year goal is that 100% of submissions get a registered owner and an answer within N working days, with submitted and implemented counts tracked monthly. This addresses the owner's stated fear (silent loss, people going quiet) directly.
- **Gazprom Neft as the cautionary tale.** Its portal implemented only 290 of 4,781 ideas, even with 96% registration. Wide reach is not enough without decision throughput. For Modus Trade, the decision bottleneck is the owner and department heads, so the product should make their queue and overdue items unavoidable: a digest, escalation to the owner after the deadline, and a weekly review list.
- **Two tracks fit Modus Trade's mix.** Its departments raise both operational issues (service, purchasing, accounting) and improvement ideas. Like EVRAZ and the unnamed holding, it can run a fast track for issues and small ideas, decided by the department head within a few days, and a slow track for ideas needing budget or the owner, with a simple business-case field. Skip the effect calculators.
- **Local credibility for the pitch.** The examples closest to home are ERG (Kazakhstan, «Фабрика идей», tens of thousands of ideas, rewards and an improvement fund), KEGOC, and KazTransOil's Pavlodar division (a 38.45M KZT/yr saving from one engineer's idea). The HSE finding that only 11% of Russian employees voiced any idea in three years (snippet; no Kazakhstan equivalent found) supports the "people stop speaking up" risk.

### Gaps
- No Kazakhstan SME or B2B-distributor case with metrics was found, so the transfer argument relies on analogy with foreign SMEs and Russian or Kazakh industrial programs.
- No controlled evidence on which specific SLA length (2, 5 or 10 days) maximizes participation. The 2–5 working-day range reflects observed regulations, not tested optima.
